# New York JFK, 13 Jan 2023 — AAL 106 / DAL 1943 (Category B, pilot deviation)

**Role in the project:** counterexample. Marks the boundary of what a communications-only system can
detect. Required by the course's Minimum Evidence Standard ("at least one analysed failure or
counterexample").

**Source:** NTSB DCA23LA125, final report abstract released 4 Jun 2024,
https://www.ntsb.gov/news/press-releases/Pages/NR20240604a.aspx ; investigation page
https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA23LA125.aspx

## What happened

- ~20:44 local. ATC cleared the American 777 (AAL 106, London-bound) to **cross runway 31L at
  taxiway K**.
- The crew, distracted by interruptions and multitasking during taxi, instead **crossed runway 4L at
  taxiway J**, about 20 s after a Delta 737 (DAL 1943) had begun its takeoff roll on 4L.
- **ASDE-X** in the tower issued aural and visual alerts. Five seconds later the controller cancelled
  Delta's takeoff clearance; the 737 rejected the takeoff from ~121 mph as the 777 crossed ahead.
- Probable cause (NTSB): flight-crew distraction and loss of positional awareness; the captain was
  confused about the taxi/takeoff instructions and the first officer lost track of the airplane's
  position.

## What the engine would see

| # | Speaker | Transmission (paraphrase) | Event |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GND/TWR | American 106, cross runway 31L at kilo | `ClearToCross(AAL106, 31L)` |
| 2 | AAL | cross 31L at kilo, American 106 | `ReadbackObserved` — consistent |
| 3 | TWR | Delta 1943, runway 4L, cleared for takeoff | `ClearTakeoff(DAL1943, 4L)` |
| 4 | DAL | cleared for takeoff 4L, Delta 1943 | `ReadbackObserved` — consistent |

Runway 31L holds a `CROSS` for AAL106; runway 4L holds a `TAKEOFF` for DAL1943. Different runways:
**no conflict**. The readbacks are correct. Nothing on frequency is wrong; the aircraft simply did not
do what it read back. The engine raises no alert, and it is right not to: the transcript contains no
evidence of a conflict.

## Why this matters for the argument

- It separates two failure classes: **clearance conflicts** (controller issues incompatible
  clearances; Austin) and **compliance failures** (correct clearance, wrong execution; JFK). A
  communications-derived check addresses only the first, plus the subset of the second that shows up
  as a wrong readback.
- JFK was caught *because* JFK is one of the 44 US airports with ASDE-X/ASSC. Austin was not caught
  *because* AUS was not. The two cases together are the argument for where a voice-only tool adds
  value (the ~90% of towered airports without surface surveillance) and where it cannot substitute
  for surveillance.
- For the report's "alternative explanation" requirement: a reader may object that the tool would
  miss most incursions, since FY2024 incursions were 62% pilot deviations. The response is the
  severity split: 59% of Category A incursions since FY2022 were controller operational incidents
  (`ab-incursions-fy2022-2024.csv`), and the ASRS triage pass estimates how many pilot deviations
  leave a trace on frequency.
