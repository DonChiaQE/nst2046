# LaGuardia, 22 Mar 2026 — ACA 8646 / Rescue 35 (fatal accident, operational incident)

**Role in the project:** primary positive case study for the proposal. The conflicting clearances
were both on frequency two minutes before impact, and the surface-surveillance safety net was
present but blind.

**Source:** NTSB investigation DCA26MA161 (ongoing; preliminary report 23 Apr 2026).
https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA26MA161.aspx — preliminary report PDF:
https://data.ntsb.gov/carol-repgen/api/Aviation/ReportMain/GenerateNewestReport/202674/pdf
(save as `data/incidents/ntsb-DCA26MA161-lga-2026-03-22-prelim.pdf`, git-ignored).
Facts below are from the preliminary report as reported by AVweb, CBC and FireRescue1; verify
against the PDF before quoting in the report.

## What happened (from the preliminary report)

- Night, 23:37 EDT. Jazz Aviation CRJ900 (C-GNJZ), operating as Air Canada Express 8646 from
  Montreal, landing runway 4.
- 23:35:07 — local controller cleared ACA 8646 to land on runway 4.
- 23:37:04 — local controller cleared "Truck 1 and company" (seven ARFF vehicles responding to an
  unrelated emergency) to cross runway 4 at taxiway D. The aircraft was ~130 ft AGL, ~¼ mile out.
- Seconds later: "Stop, stop, stop, stop, Truck 1, stop, stop, stop." The truck crews heard it but
  did not know it was for them.
- The aircraft struck Rescue 35 (Oshkosh Striker) on the landing roll. Both pilots killed; 39 of 76
  occupants taken to hospital, 6 serious.
- **ASDE-X was installed and operating but did not alert.** None of the vehicles carried
  transponders, the system could not resolve the group as separate targets, and so could not
  correlate the aircraft track with Rescue 35's path.
- Staffing: two controllers on position, each working two combined positions; two further
  controllers were in the tower off duty. FAA has since moved to dismiss two controllers who left
  early.

| 1 | 23:35:07 | TWR | Air Canada 8646, runway 4, cleared to land | `ClearToLand(ACA8646, 04)` |
| 2 | | ACA | cleared to land runway 4, Air Canada 8646 | `ReadbackObserved` — consistent |
| 3 | 23:37:04 | TWR | Truck 1 and company, cross runway 4 at Delta | `ClearCross(TRUCK1, 04)` |
| 4 | 23:37:0x | TWR | Stop, stop, stop, Truck 1, stop | (revocation; too late) |

At step 3 the engine holds an active `LAND` reservation for ACA8646 on 04 and receives a `CROSS`
on the same runway. Matrix cell CROSS (new) vs LAND (active) = **alert**, ~2 minutes after the
landing clearance and at the moment the crossing clearance is issued.

## What this case shows, and what it does not

- Unlike Austin, this is not an anticipated-separation judgement call. A landing clearance and a
  crossing clearance for the same runway, with the aircraft on short final, is unconditionally
  conflicting. No distance information is needed to flag it, so this is not the engine's structural
  false-alarm cell.
- The surveillance net existed and failed for a data reason (no transponders on the vehicles). A
  text-level check does not depend on what the surveillance can see; it depends on what was said.
- "Truck 1 and company" is non-standard phraseology and the callsign is a vehicle, not an aircraft.
  Rule-based extraction must have a vehicle-callsign pattern to catch it; this is exactly the
  robustness gap H1 predicts the LLM arm closes. Whether it also emits phantom clearances (H2) is
  the empirical question.
- The controller's own revocation came within seconds, so the alert would have arrived at roughly
  the same time as the controller's own realisation. The case argues for the check's *validity*,
  not for a large lead-time advantage. Say this honestly in the report.
- Staffing (two controllers, four positions, ~midnight) is a contributing factor in the same family
  as the 2023 NAS Safety Review Team findings; cite if the staffing angle is kept.
