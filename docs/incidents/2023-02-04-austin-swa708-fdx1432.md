# Austin-Bergstrom, 4 Feb 2023 — SWA 708 / FDX 1432 (Category A, operational incident)

**Role in the project:** positive case study. The archetype the engine is built to flag.

**Source:** NTSB Aviation Investigation Report AIR-24-02 (adopted 6 Jun 2024), local copy
`data/incidents/ntsb-AIR-24-02-austin-2023-02-04.pdf` (git-ignored; download from
https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/AIR2402.pdf).

## What happened (from the executive summary)

- Dense fog; RVR-limited operations. The tower could not see the taxiway or runway.
- The local controller cleared the SWA 737 for takeoff on runway 18L and instructed the FedEx 767,
  on approach to 18L, to continue its approach to the same runway.
- FAA procedure required 2-mile separation. When the SWA airplane lined up on the 18L centreline
  and stopped for an engine run-up (19 s), the FedEx airplane was 1.5 miles out.
- The FedEx crew saw the SWA airplane's outline through the fog and went around. Closest point:
  150-170 ft, less than the length of the 767.
- AUS had **no surface detection equipment** (ASDE-X/ASSC). The NTSB lists that as the first safety
  issue and recommends surface detection at all airports without it.

## What the NTSB found

- The controller had "an inaccurate mental model" of the SWA airplane's position: he assumed it was
  at the hold-short line when it was 550 ft away, and assumed it would depart before FedEx arrived.
- The controller did not verify the SWA airplane's position when it requested takeoff clearance.
- The SWA crew knew traffic was on short final but did not tell the controller they would stop on the
  runway for an engine run-up (not required, but "would have been prudent").
- No recent low-visibility or SMGCS training at the AUS tower.

## Clearance sequence (as the engine would see it)

Reconstructed from the report; times approximate. To be replaced with the ATC transcript from the
docket (ATC Group Chair's Factual Report) once transcribed by the team.

| # | Speaker | Transmission (paraphrase) | Event |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | TWR | FedEx 1432, runway 18L, cleared to land | `ClearToLand(FDX1432, 18L)` |
| 2 | FDX | cleared to land 18L, FedEx 1432 | `ReadbackObserved` — consistent |
| 3 | SWA | Southwest 708, ready to go, 18L | (request, `NoOp`) |
| 4 | TWR | Southwest 708, runway 18L, cleared for takeoff, traffic three-mile final | `ClearTakeoff(SWA708, 18L)` |
| 5 | SWA | cleared for takeoff 18L, Southwest 708 | `ReadbackObserved` — consistent |
| 6 | FDX | Tower, confirm cleared to land 18L, FedEx 1432 | query |
| 7 | TWR | FedEx 1432, affirmative, cleared to land / continue | re-affirms LAND |
| 8 | FDX | Southwest abort / FedEx is on the go | `GoAround(FDX1432)` |

At step 4 the engine holds an active `LAND` reservation for FDX1432 on 18L and receives a `TAKEOFF`
for SWA708 on the same runway. Matrix cell TAKEOFF (new) vs LAND (active) = **alert**. The engine
flags the conflict at the moment the takeoff clearance is issued, ~3 minutes before the closest point.

## What this case shows, and what it does not

- Both readbacks were correct. Readback checking alone (SCOPE, HAAWAII) would not have flagged this.
  A cross-aircraft runway ledger does.
- The takeoff clearance was **legal** under anticipated separation; it became hazardous because the
  2-mile gap was never real and the controller could not see. A voice-only engine cannot know the
  distance. So the same matrix cell that catches Austin will also fire on every routine, legal
  "cleared for takeoff, traffic on final" — this is the engine's structural false-alarm source, and it
  is a design decision (see the evaluation addendum) whether to keep it at `alert`.
- The engine's alert is precisely the prompt the NTSB says was missing: verify the departing
  aircraft's position before relying on anticipated separation.
