# Cross-arm replication of the divergence analysis (08 Nov 2026)

Generated by `scripts/cross_arm_replication.py` from the stored evaluation rows (`data/eval/conflicts/*.jsonl`) and the two label sheets. No database and no API call, so this reproduces anywhere the repo is checked out.

The report's Appendix A inspects rules vs Sonnet 5 on human transcripts. This asks whether the same story holds for a second model (Haiku 5.5) and a second transcript kind (ASR).

## 3. Mechanism behind every divergent positive

Classified by `classify()` from the two parties to a RUNWAY conflict: the *trigger*, the injected conflicting clearance, and the *prior holder*, the reservation already open on that runway. `engine.apply` caps the conflict at `warning` when either party's callsign is unknown, so an arm that resolves a callsign the other leaves blank catches a conflict the other only warns about — on the holder (`identity (holder)`, which needs identity carried across transmissions) or on the trigger itself (`identity (trigger)`, which does not). `supersession` is one aircraft given two kinds of hold, where the later invented one rewrites the earlier and the matrix rates the rewritten pair lower. `unclassified` is counted rather than explained away.

| Comparison | Caught by rules only | Caught by LLM only | identity (holder) | identity (trigger) | supersession | unclassified |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| rules vs Sonnet 5 / gold | 2 | 5 | 5 | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| rules vs Haiku 5.5 / gold | 2 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| rules vs Sonnet 5 / ASR | 2 | 6 | 5 | 1 | 2 | 0 |
